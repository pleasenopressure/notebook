## overfitting
Overfitting is when a model performs very well on the training set but generalises poorly to new data, because it has learned noise rather than the underlying pattern. A common remedy is **pruning / early stopping**, using a validation set or cross-validation to choose a simpler model that generalises better.

It provide a partical test for intelligence based on **observable** conversational **behavior**, without needing to inspect its internal **mechanisms**.

- It require a wide range of human cognitive abilities (language, knowledge,reasoning) to pass the test.

### Turing Test — 2 limitations

- **Imitation ≠ understanding:** a system may pass by **manipulating** symbols or using tricks, without **genuine** semantic understanding.**模仿≠理解：** 一个系统可以通过操纵符号或使用技巧来蒙混过关，而无需真正理解语义。
    
- **Human-centric & narrow interface:** it focuses on human-like text **dialogue** and may ignore embodied/perceptual intelligence or other **non-linguistic** forms of cognition. **以人为中心且界面狭窄：** 它专注于类似人类的文本对话，可能会忽略具身/感知智能或其他非语言认知形式。   
- **The "Lady Lovelace" Objection:** Machines only do what we program them to do; they cannot **originate** anything new.

### Ethics — existential vs societal risks (1 example each)

- **Existential risk (example):** loss of human control from highly capable, misaligned systems that could cause catastrophic outcomes at a civilisation scale. **生存风险（例如）：** 人类对高度强大但失衡的系统失去控制，可能导致文明层面的灾难性后果。
    

- **Societal risk (example):** biased or unfair decisions (e.g., hiring/credit/policing) or large-scale misinformation that harms individuals/groups and undermines trust. **社会风险（例如）：** 有偏见或不公平的决定（例如，招聘/信贷/警务）或大规模虚假信息，损害个人/群体并破坏信任。
