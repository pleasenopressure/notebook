I read a DIS 2025 paper about how robot gestures influence interpersonal perception in group conversations. They found that when a robot nodded while one person was speaking, other group members perceived that person as more sociable and supportive, although they did not rate the actual contribution as better.

This gave me an idea for my questionnaire. Besides measuring conversation flow and the robot's intervention quality, I think I should also measure whether the robot changes how the two participants perceive each other—for example, whether they feel the other person is interested in their opinions, supportive, and easy to talk to.

I also read the Micbot paper, which explores whether a peripheral robot can shape group conversation without becoming an active speaker. The robot simply turned toward the current speaker and occasionally leaned toward the least active participant. They found that it did not significantly change speaking time or speaking turns, but it made backchanneling more evenly distributed, and more balanced backchanneling was associated with better team performance.

This suggests that, for my questionnaire, I should not only measure how much people talked, but also whether participants felt more engaged, listened to each other more, participated more evenly, and whether the robot’s intervention felt subtle rather than distracting.
